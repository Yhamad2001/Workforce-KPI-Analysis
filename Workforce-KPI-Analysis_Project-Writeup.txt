OVERVIEW
--------
For this project I wanted to build something that takes scattered data across
departments and turns it into something leadership at a company can actually use to make
decisions. I decided to focus on workforce operations, specifically tracking
employee attendance, overtime, and performance reviews across a 3-month period.

The end result was a normalized MySQL database with 4 relational tables and a
6-tab Excel KPI dashboard that surfaces department-level insights with
conditional formatting, pivot tables, and charts.

The dashboard tracks 15 employees across 5 departments — Dispatch, Finance,
HR, Logistics, and Operations — with a total estimated annual payroll of
$789,340 and $2,874.76 in overtime costs recorded over the analysis period.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — DATABASE DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first thing I did was design the database schema. I knew I needed to keep
the data normalized so nothing was duplicated and every table had a clear
purpose. I ended up with 4 tables:

  employees
  ---------
  This is the central table everything else joins to. I stored the core
  employee details here — name, department, job title, hire date, hourly rate,
  employment type (full-time or part-time), manager ID, and region. I gave each
  employee a unique employee_id as the primary key. The dataset includes 15
  employees — 13 full-time and 2 part-time — spread across 5 departments.

  attendance
  ----------
  This table tracks daily clock-in and clock-out for each employee over 3
  months (January through March 2024). Each row is one day for one employee.
  I included a status column — Present, Absent, Late, or Half-Day — so I could
  filter and aggregate without having to calculate status from the times every
  time I ran a query.

  performance_reviews
  -------------------
  Quarterly review scores for each employee on a 1–5 scale. I captured both
  Q1 and Q2 2024 so I could compare score trends and identify who improved
  quarter over quarter. All 14 reviewed employees showed improvement from
  Q1 to Q2.

  overtime_log
  ------------
  Weekly overtime hours per employee, including who approved the overtime. I
  kept this separate from attendance because overtime is tracked by week, not
  by day, and the aggregation logic is different. The dataset covers 6
  employees across 3 departments who logged overtime during the period.

I wrote all the table creation and data insertion in a single SQL script
(Workforce-KPI-Analysis_create_database.sql) so anyone running the project
can set up the full database in one step.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — SQL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Once the database was set up I wrote the analysis queries. I organized them
into 5 sections so they matched the tabs I was planning to build in Excel:

  Section 1 — Headcount & Department Summary
  -------------------------------------------
  I started with headcount by department broken down by employment type, then
  calculated an estimated annual payroll for each department using a simple
  CASE statement — full-time employees at 2,080 hours per year and part-time
  at 1,040. The results showed Operations as the highest-cost department at
  $199,420 annually, followed by Dispatch at $177,840 and Logistics at
  $174,720. Finance and HR came in lower at $125,840 and $112,320 respectively.
  I also calculated tenure for each employee using DATEDIFF so I could see
  how long people had been with the company.

  Section 2 — Attendance & Reliability KPIs
  ------------------------------------------
  This is where I aggregated the attendance data. I used conditional SUM to
  count present days, absent days, and late days per employee, then calculated
  attendance rate as a percentage. The overall average attendance rate came out
  to 85% across the tracked employees. The pivot breakdown showed Operations
  had the most absences (3) and late arrivals (5), while Dispatch had the
  best record with zero absences and zero late arrivals in the period.

  I also wrote a flag query that classifies employees by status — anyone
  below 80% gets flagged as REVIEW, 80–90% as Monitor, and above 90% as Good.
  In my dataset, Damon Price came in at 66.7% which flagged him for review,
  while Layla Hassan sat at exactly 80% in the Monitor band. James Osei and
  Chloe Martin both came in above 90%.

  One query I was particularly happy with uses TIMESTAMPDIFF to calculate
  average hours worked per day per employee from the raw clock-in and
  clock-out times.

  Section 3 — Overtime Analysis
  ------------------------------
  I calculated total overtime hours and cost per employee — overtime is paid at
  1.5x the hourly rate, so the cost formula is: overtime_hours * hourly_rate *
  1.5. The results showed James Osei leading in hours at 21.5 with a cost of
  $580.50, while Priya Kapoor had the highest dollar cost at $720.00 despite
  fewer hours — because of her higher $30/hr rate.

  At the department level, Logistics carried the heaviest overtime burden at
  37.5 hours and $1,300.50 in cost. Operations came second at 28.5 hours and
  $930.38, and Dispatch was lowest at 18 hours and $643.88. Total overtime
  cost across the period was $2,874.76.

  The weekly trend data showed the heaviest overtime week was the first week
  of January with 19 total hours across 4 employees, while the lightest was
  the third week of January at just 2 hours from a single employee.

  A cross-join analysis between the overtime and attendance tables helped me
  identify employees who had both high overtime hours and absences in the same
  period — a potential burnout indicator worth flagging for management.

  Section 4 — Performance Review Analysis
  ----------------------------------------
  I joined the performance_reviews table to calculate average scores by
  department per quarter. The strongest performing department in Q2 was
  Dispatch at 4.27, followed by HR at 4.20 and Finance at 4.00. Logistics
  and Operations came in at 3.83 and 4.07 respectively.

  Every single employee improved their score from Q1 to Q2 — no declines and
  no flat scores. Priya Kapoor showed the largest individual jump at +0.4
  points (3.9 to 4.3). Layla Hassan, Sofia Nguyen, Chloe Martin, and Owen
  Murphy each improved by +0.3 points. The remaining 9 employees all improved
  by +0.2 points.

  The scatter chart pairing attendance rate with average performance score
  showed an interesting pattern — it wasn't a clean linear relationship.
  Damon Price had the lowest attendance at 66.7% but one of the higher
  performance scores at 4.5, while Chloe Martin had perfect 100% attendance
  but a score of 3.75. This suggested attendance and performance aren't as
  directly correlated in this dataset as you might assume, which is worth
  noting honestly.

  Section 5 — Executive Summary View
  ------------------------------------
  The final query is a single one-row-per-department rollup that combines
  headcount, average hourly rate, attendance percentage, average performance
  score, and total overtime hours. Operations led in both overtime hours (855)
  and average performance score (4.18), while Logistics had the highest
  overtime hours relative to its size with 677 hours across 4 employees.

All queries are in Workforce-KPI-Analysis_queries.sql, organized by section
with comments explaining what each query does and which Excel tab it feeds.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — EXCEL DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I exported the query results as CSV files from MySQL Workbench and imported
them into Excel using Data → From Text/CSV to keep the columns clean. I built
the workbook with 6 tabs in this order:

  Tab 1 — Raw Data
  ----------------
  This tab is the data store. I pasted all 4 query result sets here with
  section labels (EMPLOYEES, ATTENDANCE, OVERTIME, PERFORMANCE) and 3 blank
  rows between each section. I never formatted this tab — no colors, no merged
  cells — because all the other tabs reference it as a clean data source. I
  formatted date columns as MM/DD/YYYY, currency columns to 2 decimal places,
  and percentage columns to 1 decimal place.

  Tab 2 — Dept Summary
  ---------------------
  I pasted the headcount data (Query 1A) starting at A1 and the payroll data
  (Query 1C) starting at H1. The headcount table shows all 7 rows —
  5 departments with Operations and Logistics each split into full-time and
  part-time rows. The payroll table shows Operations as the highest at
  $199,420 and HR as the lowest at $112,320.

  I applied a green-yellow-red color scale to the avg_hourly_rate column using
  conditional formatting so high-paid roles stand out green and lower-paid
  ones show in red. I then created a clustered bar chart for headcount by
  department and a pie chart for the payroll distribution.

  Tab 3 — Attendance KPIs
  ------------------------
  I pasted the attendance summary (Query 2A) and added a helper Status column
  using this formula:

    =IF(H2<80,"⚠ REVIEW",IF(H2<90,"Monitor","✓ Good"))

  In my data, Damon Price at 66.7% flagged as REVIEW, Layla Hassan at 80.0%
  flagged as Monitor, and James Osei at 93.3% and Chloe Martin at 100% both
  flagged as Good. I applied matching conditional formatting — red, yellow,
  green — to both the percentage and status columns.

  I also built a line chart from the monthly trend data (Query 2B) showing
  absences by department. The data showed Operations consistently had the most
  absences each month, while Dispatch had zero absences in January and only
  one per month in February and March.

  The PivotTable summarizes by department: Operations had 3 absences and 5
  late arrivals with a 73.3% average attendance, Logistics had 0 absences and
  1 late arrival at 93.3%, and Dispatch had 0 absences and 0 late arrivals at
  a perfect 100%.

  Tab 4 — Overtime
  ----------------
  I pasted three query results here — per-employee overtime (Query 3A) at A1,
  department-level overtime (Query 3B) at H1, and the weekly trend (Query 3C)
  below 3B. I added an OT Category helper column using:

    =IF(E2>15,"High OT",IF(E2>8,"Moderate","Low"))

  James Osei and Priya Kapoor both classified as High OT, Layla Hassan, Damon
  Price and Chloe Martin as Moderate, and Kevin Adler as Low.

  I built a clustered bar chart from the department data showing both hours
  and cost side by side — I added a secondary axis for the cost series because
  the dollar values ($643–$1,300) are much larger than the hour values (18–37)
  and would have crushed the bars without it. I also built a line chart from
  the weekly trend data showing the overtime fluctuation across 8 weeks.

  Tab 5 — Performance
  --------------------
  I pasted the department-by-quarter scores (Query 4A) at A1 and the
  per-employee score changes (Query 4B) at H1. Before building the grouped bar
  chart I reshaped the 4A data into a pivot-style table with departments as
  rows and Q1/Q2 as separate columns — Excel needs this layout to correctly
  group the bars side by side per department. I added a trend column to the
  4B table using:

    =IF(E2>0,"Improved",IF(E2<0,"Declined","No Change"))

  Every employee showed "Improved" — no one declined or stayed flat.

  I also pasted the attendance vs performance data (Query 4D) and built a
  scatter chart with a linear trendline. Interestingly, the data doesn't show
  a clean correlation — Damon Price had the lowest attendance (66.7%) but the
  highest performance score (4.5), which suggests other factors beyond
  attendance drive performance in this team.

  Tab 6 — Summary Dashboard
  --------------------------
  This is the executive-facing tab. I hid the gridlines, set all row heights
  to 20px, and built a dark navy title banner in row 1 using merged cells. The
  banner includes a live date formula:

    ="Last Updated: "&TEXT(TODAY(),"MM/DD/YYYY")

  Below the banner I built 5 KPI cards pulling live data from the other tabs:

    Total Headcount   → 15
    Avg Attendance %  → 61.0% (average across tracked employees)
    Avg Perf Score    → 3.95 (average across all reviews)
    Total OT Cost     → $2,874.76
    Depts Tracked     → 5

  Below the cards I arranged 4 charts in a 2x2 grid — I copied each one from
  its source tab using Home → Copy as Picture and pasted them onto this tab
  as frozen images. Finally I protected the sheet using Review → Protect Sheet
  with no password, allowing only cell selection.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4 — KEY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After running all the queries and building the dashboard, a few things stood
out clearly from the actual data:

1. Logistics carried the highest overtime burden by both hours and cost.
   James Osei logged 21.5 overtime hours ($580.50) and Priya Kapoor logged
   16 hours ($720.00) — making Logistics responsible for $1,300.50 of the
   total $2,874.76 in overtime costs, or roughly 45% of all overtime spending.
   Operations came second at $930.38, and Dispatch was lowest at $643.88.

2. Operations had the worst attendance numbers. The department averaged 73.3%
   attendance — well below the overall average of 85% — with 3 total absences
   and 5 late arrivals across the tracked period. Damon Price specifically came
   in at 66.7%, the lowest of any employee in the dataset, and was flagged for
   management review. Dispatch, by contrast, had a perfect record with zero
   absences and zero late arrivals.

3. Every single employee improved their performance score from Q1 to Q2.
   Priya Kapoor led with the biggest jump at +0.4 points (3.9 to 4.3).
   Layla Hassan, Sofia Nguyen, Chloe Martin, and Owen Murphy each improved by
   +0.3 points. The remaining 9 employees improved by +0.2 points. No one
   declined. Dispatch had the highest Q2 average at 4.27, followed by HR
   at 4.20.

4. Attendance and performance don't correlate as directly as expected in this
   dataset. Damon Price had the lowest attendance at 66.7% but the highest
   individual performance score at 4.5 in the scatter data, while Chloe Martin
   had perfect 100% attendance but a score of 3.75. This is an honest finding —
   the scatter chart shows the trendline but the individual data points tell a
   more nuanced story about what drives performance in this team.

5. The payroll estimate showed Operations as the most expensive department at
   $199,420 annually, driven by a mix of full-time and part-time staff and
   the highest headcount at 4 employees. HR was the lowest at $112,320 with
   2 full-time employees. Total estimated annual payroll across all 5
   departments came to $789,340.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO REPRODUCE THIS PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Install MySQL and open MySQL Workbench
  2. Open Workforce-KPI-Analysis_create_database.sql
     Uncomment the first 2 lines and run the full file
  3. Verify with: SELECT COUNT(*) FROM employees; — should return 15
  4. Open Workforce-KPI-Analysis_queries.sql
     Add USE workforce_analysis; at the top
     Run each section and export results as CSV
  5. Open Excel, create workforce_dashboard.xlsx with 6 tabs
     Follow Workforce-KPI-Analysis_excel_instructions.txt tab by tab
  6. Open workforce_dashboard.xlsx to view the finished dashboard
